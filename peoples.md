# Peoples

## The races
- **Six starting races: human, dwarf, halfling, high elf, dark elf, orc.** CANON (TCG)
- **Others may come later** if the lore fits; undead is the example given. DRAFT (TCG)
- **Races should feel like real peoples, not generic units.** CANON (TCG)
- **Each race has its own name for itself** in its own tongue. The games use human, dwarf and the rest as the translation, with native names as flavour. DRAFT. The names wait for the naming session (Q15).

## Independence and politics
- **The races are fiercely independent.** Even allies only *tolerate* each other. Some are not allied at all and don't allow other races into their capitals unescorted. CANON (TCG)
- **Each race governs itself.** CANON. The only examples of political form are "human kingdoms" and "orc chieftains". DRAFT (TCG)
- **No race-wide alliances.** Each kingdom, clan or house makes its own. The common pattern: humans, dwarves and halflings are often allied through trade; elves tolerate humans at arm's length; high and dark elves are not allied with each other; orcs make no formal alliances, but individual orcs hire out to anyone. DRAFT
- **Present-day conflicts, all regional** (DRAFT):
  - Humans: rival kingdoms dispute the unclaimed wilds; beasts press out of those wilds; tension over mages at home (Witch Hunters).
  - Dwarves: holding mountain passes against what comes from below and against raiders; clan disputes over mines and runes.
  - Halflings: small farming peoples caught between larger neighbours; many live on land granted by human crowns.
  - High elves: an internal rift over war-magic, since exiles like Vaelith; old enmity with dark elves.
  - Dark elves: intrigue between rival houses (assassins, informants); old enmity with high elves.
  - Orcs: rival chieftains feud for land and standing; some clans raid borders, many sell their swords.
- **Rare exception:** a human crown can grant land inside its kingdom to an allied race (dwarves, halflings, elves), which rules it by its own customs. DRAFT. See `human-kingdoms.md`.
- **The world is vast.** There are no world-spanning factions. One kingdom may know of another on the far side of the world only through rumor, and as strangers they are more likely hostile than allied. CANON

## Aptitude for Magic and Martial
- **Magic aptitude is partly in the blood:** some races are more likely to produce Magic users. CANON (TCG)
- **Any race can produce Martial fighters**, but culture and available knowledge make some races lean Martial. CANON (TCG)
- **Magic among humans:** some humans are born with the gift and some are not, possibly because of ancestry from races more likely to produce Magic users. This implies mixed ancestry between races. DRAFT (idea)
- **Which races lean which way** (DRAFT):
  - Magic in the blood, strongest first: high elf, dark elf, orc and human (varies by line), halfling, dwarf.
  - Martial by culture: dwarves, orcs and humans strongest.
  - Dwarven runecraft is the Magic of their rare gifted. Halflings have small folk magic (hedge witches).
  - Orcs honour their storm-callers, so the stigma against mages is weakest among orcs and strongest among humans and dwarves.

## Where they live
- **On still-blade's continent:** humans (several kingdoms), dwarves (in the mountain range), halflings (river farmland, some on crown grants), orcs (beyond the inland wilds). High and dark elves live on other continents, known there through sea trade and rumour. DRAFT

## How projects treat race
- **TCG:** race is background only (art, name style, a small note on the card), never part of a card's or Champion's name, and there are no race-synergy mechanics. CANON (TCG)
- **RPG:** the player selects from a list of available races at character creation (see `specializations.md`). CANON. The prototype currently has only human models (male in use, female in progress). DRAFT (RPG)
- **still-blade:** the player character is always human. CANON (a per-game rule, tied to a humans-only tournament; see `projects.md`). NPCs are human only for now; other races possibly later. DRAFT
