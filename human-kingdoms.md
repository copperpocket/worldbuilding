# Human Kingdoms

How human kingdoms are organized. Other races govern themselves in their own ways (see `peoples.md`). Everything here is DRAFT unless marked, and every title is a PLACEHOLDER as a name. Source: worldbuilding sessions, 2026-11-15.

## Two kinds of word
- **Political words** say who a place belongs to: kingdom, province, seat, capital, grant.
- **Place words** say how big it is: city, town, village, hamlet, fort or outpost.
- A place can hold a political role; a town can be a province's seat.

## Settlements, largest to smallest
| Place | What it is | Led by |
|---|---|---|
| **City** | Large, walled, many trades. Rare, often one per province; usually a capital or seat | **Consul** |
| **Town** | A real market, craftspeople, a guard | **Reeve** (appointed by the Duke or Duchess) |
| **Village** | Farming or fishing, a small market at most | **Elder** (chosen locally) |
| **Hamlet** | A few households, no market, no church | No ruler; answers to the nearest village |
| **Fort or outpost** | A garrison | A military leader (see Military) |

- A lone dwelling is a **homestead**.
- Most settlements are towns or villages. Large or rich towns may have a **Town Moot** (guild heads and elders) advising the Reeve; a village may have an elders' circle.
- A settlement that follows no standard is allowed, for example from distance or isolation.

## Political levels
- **Kingdom:** ruled by a **King or Queen**; the heir is a **Crown Prince or Crown Princess**. A kingdom holds several provinces and never covers a whole continent unless by a rare exception. CANON
- **Province:** a large region of a kingdom, ruled by a **Duke or Duchess** the crown appoints, usually from a noble family. "Province" is the neutral word; a **duchy** is a province ruled by a Duke or Duchess; a **county** is a smaller region ruled by a **Count or Countess** (kept in reserve).
- **Seat:** the settlement where a province's ruler lives and the province is run from. Normally a city; a remote province without a city could use a town.
- **Capital:** the kingdom's main city, where the crown sits. It lies in a province with its own Duke or Duchess, who lives in a second city as the seat. The crown and the Duke or Duchess both carry out their normal duties.
- **Who appoints whom:** the crown appoints the Duke or Duchess; the Duke or Duchess appoints town heads and fort commanders; villages choose locally; a hamlet answers to the nearest village.
- **The royal family is small:** the crown, the consort (Queen Consort or Prince Consort), the children (Prince or Princess), the monarch's siblings and parents (a widowed ruling parent is a Dowager). Everyone else is **kin of the crown** and holds noble titles, so a Duke or Duchess is often a close kinsman.
- **Allied grants:** the crown can rarely grant land inside a kingdom to an allied race (dwarves, halflings, elves). They rule it by their own customs and owe the crown allegiance.
- **Written and spoken forms:** the title comes before the name, with "of [place]" for formal use ("Duke Corin Hale of Greywater"). Titles are not gendered except King, Queen, Prince, Princess, Duke, Duchess, Count and Countess. Honorifics such as "Your Majesty" and "Your Highness" are suggestions.

## The city council
Every city has a council led by the **Consul**, with up to seven seats: **Consul, Protector** (the city's whole military), **Chamberlain** (coin and taxes), **Magistrate** (law), **Guildmaster** (the trades), **Harbormaster** (the port, only in a port city), **Speaker** (ordinary residents). Which seats exist is up to each city.
- **Seat city:** the Duke or Duchess rules the province and holds the city; the council reports to the Consul, who runs the city for them.
- **Chartered city:** a rare second city in a province, with a crown charter. The council governs by vote, the Consul chairs and breaks ties, and the Duke or Duchess and the crown can overrule.

## Military
**Rank order, top down:** **Marshal** (the whole army), **Warden** (a border region and its forts), **Protector** (a city's whole military), the branch leaders, then **Captain, Sergeant, Veteran, Soldier, Recruit**.
- **Appointments:** the crown appoints the Marshal and the Wardens (Wardens on the Marshal's advice).
- **Commanding ranks:** Sergeant is the lowest, leading a squad of about 10. Veteran, Soldier and Recruit command no one; a Soldier can rise to Veteran without ever leading.
- **City military:** one Protector commands the city guard (a **Commander**), the castle guard (a **Castellan**) and any other guard in the city. The Protector answers to the Consul, and to a Warden when required. The full structure applies to the capital and seat cities; other cities have a Protector alone, adding a Castellan only with a castle and a Commander only with a separate watch. A town's guard is a Captain under the Reeve. A village has no guard or a militia under the Elder.
- **Branches and leaders:** city guard, field army, mounted, siege and works: **Commander**. Castle or major fort: **Castellan**. Frontier: **Warden**. Fleet: **Admiral**. Royal guard (in the capital): the Protector directly. Reuse of a title across branches is fine. Every branch ranks Captain through Recruit beneath its leader.
- **Knights** are an honour from the crown, not a rank. The crown keeps a register and calls on its knights; they swear on accepting the title to answer. Any rank can be a knight. Address: "Ser". 
- Open: whether a Castellan outranks a Commander (Q57), the fleet's lower ranks (Q58), and mages in a kingdom's military (Q59).

## The grand tournament (still-blade)
- **Held by a human kingdom**, only for humans. Who runs it and where its ground is are not defined (see `locations.md`).
- **Two tournaments:** the grand tournament, where gear matters and Attackers win most of the time, with rare players from other paths winning in creative ways; and a lesser tournament that counts only landed line attacks (no damage, no E skills) so anyone can compete.
- **Rules:** both are single-elimination brackets at a ground with several rings, watched by spectators. The grand tournament is one open bracket for any level with exactly one champion, held rarely (weekly or monthly, undecided). Winners earn tournament marks, spent on upgrade materials, and the champion holds a title until the next one. The marks and the title are PLACEHOLDER.
